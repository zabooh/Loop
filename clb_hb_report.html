CLB Half-Bridge - Hardware Test Report

Device: PIC16F13145 Curiosity Nano  |  Analyzer: Saleae Logic 8  |  Date: 2026-06-16 01:03  |  Sample rate: 100 MS/s

1. What this peripheral is, and what it can do

A complementary half-bridge driver on RC0 (high-side, HS) and RC1 (low-side, LS) of the PIC16F13145. The part has no CWG, so the function is built from the on-chip fabric:

  • CLB (Configurable Logic Block) - a free-running counter generates the PWM carrier; two octave taps give the selectable frequency.
  • TMR2-HLT - an edge-triggered monostable, retriggered by every PWM edge, that times the dead-time (T2PR = dt ticks of the 32 MHz FOSC clock).
  • 2x CLC D-flip-flops - turn PWM + the monostable pulse into the complementary, dead-timed HS / LS. HS can only be high while PWM=1, LS only while PWM=0, so non-overlap is guaranteed by construction - not by timing margins.

Achievable capabilities

Frequency: 2 settings - 125 kHz / 62.5 kHz Dead-time: 0 - 255 ticks x 31.25 ns = 0 - ~7.97 us Dead-time step: 31.25 ns Shoot-through: none (by construction)

Frequency and dead-time are set independently and live over the serial console; the dead-time is generated from FOSC and is therefore the same at either frequency.

3. Test procedure

The device is reset once (to clear any sticky fabric state), then for every combination of the two frequencies and the dead-time set {2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64} ticks the firmware is commanded (clb off; clb freq c; clb dt n; clb on) and both outputs are captured on the Saleae at 100 MS/s. From the transition stream the analyzer measures, per setting: the switching frequency (HS rising-edge rate), the dead-time (median of the both-low gaps), the shoot-through (any both-high time), the HS duty and the number of dead-time gaps sampled (308919 gaps total across the sweep).

5. Assessment

5.1 Dead-time accuracy

Across the whole sweep the measured dead-time tracks the commanded value dt x 31.25 ns with a worst-case deviation of 10 ns - i.e. within about 0.3 tick(s) of the 31.25 ns step. The relation is linear and monotonic (plot 4.1), and the error is dominated by the 100 MS/s sampling grid (10 ns) plus the synchronizer latency, not by the design. The dead-time is identical at both frequencies, confirming it is generated from FOSC and decoupled from the carrier.

5.2 Non-overlap (shoot-through)

Worst-case both-high time across all 12 settings: 0 ns (none detected - non-overlap holds at every setting). Because HS is gated by PWM=1 and LS by PWM=0, the two can never be commanded high together; this is a structural guarantee, which the measurement confirms.

5.3 Frequency accuracy

Measured carrier: 124.89 kHz (nominal 125, -0.09 %) and 62.47 kHz (nominal 62.5, -0.06 %). The taps are exact integer divisions of BLE_clk (FOSC/256 and FOSC/512); the residual is the HFINTOSC tolerance (spec +/-2 %), so the few-tenths-of-a-percent seen here is the oscillator, not quantisation.

Design verdict. The half-bridge delivers a finely and linearly adjustable dead-time (31.25 ns steps, accurate to ~1 tick) with guaranteed non-overlap, at two octave-spaced carrier frequencies. Frequency granularity is the one limitation - the CLB fabric routes only a small fixed-tap counter, so the carrier is octave-stepped rather than continuous (continuous frequency is available in the separate clbf mode, at the cost of a fixed dead-time). For a half-bridge, the fine, live dead-time is the parameter that matters most, and it is excellent.
